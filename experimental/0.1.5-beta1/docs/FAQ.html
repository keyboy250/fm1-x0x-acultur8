    What is different in this build?

    The 909 processes each active sound strip in blocks instead of making a separate processor call for every sample. The existing filters and limiter DSP, pan, sends and voice order are preserved. There is no new persistent RAM or pool allocation. This is the isolated batching optimization, not the rejected earlier mono specialization.

    What has actually been tested?

    Local checks include bit-exact audio, state and meters across 2.65 million frames, sanitizers and buffer canaries, 125 engine checks across 36 scenarios, target resource gates and updater simulations. One FM-1 was installed and its identity independently verified; the exported current project and all 25 strip configurations were preserved. Short and requested five-minute clap-strip-ON runs added no dropouts, and brief quiet listening sounded normal. The load guard remained active. These checks do not qualify every sound combination, MIDI route, recovery case or long session.

    Can I enable all effects without limits?

    No guaranteed effect count is established. Heavy combinations of active strips, voices and effects may cause audio glitches. Reduce processing and check PERF for new dropouts. The count is cumulative: compare before and after a test. Its PEAK reading is a recent display interval, not the maximum over an entire session. A visible automatic warning and priority-based load shedding remain proposals.

    Can autosave interrupt external MIDI?

    A glitch near an autosave and a new dropout were reported during editing with external MIDI. The event was not independently timestamped, but the source documents audio interruption during flash erase. The save guard checks the internal sequencer; incoming MIDI can still sound while that sequencer is stopped.

    For external MIDI performance, stop the DAW, open GLO > PROJECT > AUTOSAVE, and set it OFF with ALGORITHM. Let the final preference save finish while stopped. Save manually between takes. OFF means subsequent unsaved edits may be lost on power-off. Disabling autosave is an existing feature; no new autosave switch was added in this optimization.

    Does a sound strip add MIDI latency?

    The sound-strip processor has no lookahead delay buffer. It can reshape the attack and tail, which can change the perceived groove. The separate optional master limiter has a 64-sample lookahead; it is not the individual strip limiter.

    Separate isolated-kick recordings showed no clear added fixed attack delay with its strip ON. About 5.6–5.9 ms of attack-position standard deviation was measured across the complete MIDI/device/analog capture chain. A Reason buffer change altered recording placement, without materially reducing that variation. Digital loopback reproduced 167 attacks with a single fixed 27-sample shift and no added variation detected. This does not establish the source of the earlier variation or prove an FM-1-only timing fault. Further investigation is deferred from this experimental release; automatic latency calibration is not implemented.

    How do I sequence the device from Reason?

    Use the part's configured note channel under GLO > MIDI. Defaults are 909:10, 808:11, 303A:2, 303B:3 and BREAK:4. Notes and clock/transport are separate: sending notes does not require simultaneously running an internal pattern. Avoid concurrent internal notes if the intended test is external MIDI only.

    Can I change each drum's incoming MIDI note?

    Per-drum incoming note remapping is a recorded proposal. Current fixed mappings must be accommodated in the DAW. Use numeric MIDI note numbers because octave names vary between applications.

    Can I load a Drum Monkey MIDI clip into a device pattern?

    A direct Standard MIDI File importer is not included. A proposed converter needs mapping, destination preview and backup, and must report timing or velocity that the device pattern cannot represent. Four bars of 4/4 at sixteenth-note resolution use 64 steps; importing must also respect each drum's independent length/rate. Off-grid groove must not be silently discarded.

    Where is the CC reference?

    The matching source archive contains docs/ACULTUR8_CC_INVENTORY.json and the engine/control protocol docs. The optimization changes only inventory source hashes, not its 700 entries. Those entries include different device/browser contexts and are not a claim that 700 hardware CC numbers exist or that every mapping has physical acceptance. A release-matched human-readable guide remains a documentation target. The illustrated guide below retains the practical tested Reason examples.

    Are delay and reverb before or after compression?

    Sound → individual strip → dry mix and reverb/delay sends → effects mixed with dry → master compressor/pump → remaining master/output stages. The individual limiter shapes the effects input; master compression processes dry and effect returns together. This order is fixed. Selectable routing is a proposal, not a feature in this build.

    Does muting disable an engine and remove its CPU cost?

    No such guarantee is made. Part mute suppresses internal sequencer triggers; tails, external MIDI and shared effects can still require processing. Explicit engine enable/disable and CPU-priority policies remain proposals.

    Can I record separate drum stems?

    Individual drum mutes exist. Automated sequential MIDI-controlled stem passes and complete mute/solo restoration are proposals. Shared compression and effect tails may make isolated stems differ from a full mix. Digital loopback captures software playback; physical FM-1 audio must first enter that playback mix or a separately configured recording mix.

    What should I save before installing or reverting?

    Preserve current projects, private samples and separate reusable strip defaults. The USB current-project export excludes the reusable default bank, private sample audio and calibration. Current-project strip persistence and reusable default recall are distinct checks. Use a brief SAVE tap for the project; do not confuse Save Default with Apply Default or rewrite the saved default during recall testing.

    EXPAND is a one-way timing-format conversion. Older firmware may reject the expanded format. The provided 0.1.3 comparison baseline is not a promised overload fix; read the installation/rollback instructions before reverting. Do not erase or factory-reset to work around an unexpected format error.

    Why does the drum grid look as though every row has the same length?

    The existing shading and playhead display reuse the selected drum's length and position. This is a known display issue; rows can still have independent settings. Per-row length shading and playheads remain to be improved.

    Is this FAQ also on the device's About page?

    No. This release includes the FAQ as an offline HTML document and matching source documentation. Extending the on-device About page remains a separate proposal; this packaging pass adds no new firmware UI. Browser-only REX/slice previews described below must not be mistaken for installed hardware features.

    Why can the WAV be longer than the loop?

    The WAV packs each intact slice end to end, including its ringing tail. Marker startSample and endSample locate that sound in the bank. Marker time is its original musical onset in seconds. Adding slice lengths together would lose overlaps and gaps.

    How does sync keep the pitch?

    Play with FM1 · keep pitch schedules slice starts against the internal sequencer clock. Each slice plays at its original speed. At faster tempos tails overlap more; at slower tempos gaps may appear. This is slice sequencing, rather than continuous stretching of sustained audio.

    If stopped, both start from beat one. If already playing, the loop joins the next four-beat bar. Instrument PLAY then stops and restarts both. Original loop length is retained: a 7/8 loop still spans 3.5 quarter-note beats. Enter its source BPM if the markers have none.

    Which files belong together?

    Choose the _slices.wav and _markers.json from the same export. JSON can carry sample rate, frame count, channels, BPM, loop duration and exact boundaries. Manual import validates the available dimensions. The local converter handoff also validates the WAV checksum.

    CSV uses Slice,Start,End,Time in seconds, with an optional final Loop,,,duration row. Legacy Slice,Duration,Total CSV cannot recover the original onsets; re-export from the REX source. A rendered full-loop WAV is different from a packed slice bank.

    FM1-35 · PROPOSED · NOT IMPLEMENTED

    CV/gate sequencing: think of a guitarist’s two hands

    A guitarist combines two jobs: the picking or strumming hand supplies the rhythm, while the fretting hand helps choose the pitch through string and fret position. Our proposed split sequencer separates those jobs into two repeating lanes.

    The gate lane is the picking hand: a trigger says “play now”, a rest says “do not pick”, and a hold keeps an existing note sounding. The pitch lane is the fretting hand: it chooses the note ready for the next trigger. In this first design, changing the pitch lane alone does not start a note.

    A small example you can read like two hands

    Imagine an eight-step picking rhythm over a three-note fretting cycle: D → F → A. Both advance on the same tick in this example. The pitch updates first; a trigger then plays that pitch. The repeating pitch lane continues through rests.

    Illustration only: the proposed engine mode is not available yet.

    The dash means no new trigger, not necessarily silence: an earlier note’s decay or release can still ring. The next eight-step gate cycle starts while the three-step pitch cycle is at a different position. Their combination repeats after 24 ticks in this simple example. Different rates make the interaction more varied.

    Does moving the “fretting hand” bend a held note?

    Not in the first proposed mode: a pitch change prepares the next trigger, while the sounding note keeps its accepted pitch. A later option could let pitch changes retune a held gate, with a defined slide behaviour. Ties, gate duration, accents and slides need explicit rules and tests; the guitar metaphor does not decide them automatically.

    How would I switch it on?

    The proposal is Classic / Split Gate–Pitch for 303 A first, keeping 303 B in Classic. Classic keeps the existing combined note/rhythm sequence. Split mode would expose two rows with independent length, rate and playhead. Key/scale locks and chord-following policy would still constrain the chosen notes.

    Status: feasibility exploration FM1-35. This guide explains the design; it does not claim a working mode in the browser or on the FM1. Internal CV/gate-style sequencing uses note numbers and triggers, not physical analogue voltage outputs. Resource fit, persistence, MIDI mappings, live switching and physical testing remain pending.

    Can I save one sound and put it in another slot?

    The planned sound-variant library saves a named version of one sound: its timbre controls and processing strip. Load it into a compatible sound slot without replacing your rhythm, notes, lane length/rate, chord progression or other sounds. Pan and effect sends would be preserved by default, with an explicit option to include them.

    For example, a 303 A preset can be compatible with 303 B. An 808 kick preset cannot be treated as a 909 snare preset. The paired 808 alternatives share their existing strip. BREAK presets do not include the imported sample bank: keep the matching audio separately.

    Status: the local library prototype passes format, compatibility, export/import and overwrite-recovery tests using a simulated engine adapter. It is not connected to the emulator yet. Actual-engine capture/load, persistent browser storage, undo and save/reopen checks remain open. Browser library slots and physical FM1 flash slots are separate work. The existing PRESETS encoder still selects patterns.

    Can a bit crusher run in series with the filters and limiter?

    Yes, this is the proposed optional per-sound chain. A crusher reduces amplitude resolution and/or holds samples for a rougher texture. The low-pass filter afterwards can soften the added high frequencies; it cannot undo aliasing. Boost and the enabled limiter follow the effect and its wet/dry mix.

    Status: target FM1-37 is planned. Existing crush effects in some voices do not provide a common crusher on every strip. Bit depth, sample-hold factor, wet/dry, bypass, smooth live changes, MIDI mappings and preset storage need implementation and tests. The combined firmware has narrow measured headroom; this crusher is not yet included or resource-qualified. Browser success alone cannot establish physical-device support.

    How do I play only the 909, or turn individual parts on and off?

    On the device's HOME pattern screen, tap the first five black keys from the left to mute or unmute the five parts:

    • Black key 1: 909
    • Black key 2: 808
    • Black key 3: 303A
    • Black key 4: 303B
    • Black key 5: BREAK

    Tap the same key again to unmute. To hear only the 909's internal sequence, leave 909 unmuted and mute 808, 303A, 303B and BREAK. Check the HOME display for the current mute state: do not blindly toggle a part that is already muted. Muting preserves the notes and sound settings. These mutes suppress internal sequence triggers; they do not switch an engine off or disable incoming MIDI notes. Existing ringing tails, synth/filter state and shared effects can still consume processing time until they become idle. Use the performance display to inspect actual load rather than assuming that mute means zero CPU.

    On the LFO mixer PARTS page, black keys alone select a part; hold HOME and press its black key to mute it. On 909 MIX or 808 MIX, hold HOME and press a black key to mute that individual drum track instead. These are performance mutes; do not treat them as a saved global enable switch or a MIDI input-channel OFF setting. Ordinary performance mutes are not saved with the project; song-row mutes are separate.

    Device and Reason workflow: practical questions from testing

    Local FAQ update, 18 October 2026. These notes describe the installed 0.1.3-beta1 workflow and bounded observations from one FM1. Publication remains held. Older checkpoint sections on this page are historical; the observations here do not establish full release qualification.

    Where is the volume for an individual drum?

    Press LFO, choose 909 MIX or 808 MIX with SELECT, and select the drum with a black key. The four knobs control LEVEL, PAN, REV and DLY. Use LEVEL to balance sounds. Strip BOOST and the limiter have different jobs. The PARTS mixer provides the corresponding controls for the five whole parts. Individual drum levels were explored successfully during the physical session.

    Why do externally played bass notes repeat after release?

    Check the selected part's mixer DLY and REV sends before concluding that MIDI notes are stuck. During this session, 303B had DLY at 47%; returning it to 0 removed the repeating echoes. Reverb can leave a fading tail. A sustained or retriggering sound after tails fade still needs investigation; this example is not a universal stuck-note diagnosis.

    Can each part have its own MIDI channel?

    Yes. In GLO > MIDI, each part offers channels 1–16 or OFF. Default note channels are 909: 10; 808: 11; 303A: 2; 303B: 3; BREAK: 4. A part currently shares its assigned input/output note channel. NOTES OUT controls pattern note output, not permission to receive notes. Basic USB note response was heard for all five parts through Reason; arbitrary reassignment, full isolation, TRS and precise timing remain separate checks.

    Which notes trigger drums and slices in Reason?

    Use numeric MIDI notes when comparing software octave labels. The current drum maps are fixed. In Reason, C1 (36) triggers the kick and D1 (38) the snare on the chosen kit channel. On 909 channel 10, F-sharp1 (42) triggers closed hat and A-sharp1 (46) open hat. On 808 channel 11, G-sharp2 (56) triggers cowbell. BREAK channel 4 uses notes 36–43 for slices 1–8. These are mappings, not pitched transpositions of the drum sounds.

    In the tested Drum Monkey export, notes appeared around C3. A duplicate clip was moved down two octaves to C1 for testing. Keep the original. A whole-clip shift only solves layouts with matching relative spacing; it is not proof of universal Drum Monkey compatibility. Configurable input maps remain a suggestion.

    Will pressing Play in Reason start the FM1's own sequencer?

    Incoming notes can trigger the FM1 while its internal sequencer is stopped. MIDI Start/Continue control transport; MIDI Clock supplies timing. For a notes-only test, keep the FM1 stopped and disable Reason's Options > Sync > Send MIDI Clock. Reason's clock-host mode also sends transport messages. The FM1's CLOCK OUT setting controls its outgoing sync. Avoid assuming an incoming-sync setting from that label. In the tested setup the FM1 became quiet after Reason stopped; this is not exhaustive transport or stuck-note qualification.

    Can a Reason Combinator rotary control the FM1 without its page being open?

    Yes for the tested parameter. A separate MIDI Out on channel 6, CC106 controlled 303B Delay Send, including while another FM1 page was open. CC values are 0–127. The adjacent 303B mixer addresses are CC104 Level, CC105 Reverb Send and CC107 Pan, also on channel 6; these adjacent controls have source mappings but were not individually accepted in this physical test.

    Fixed CC control channels are independent of configurable note channels: keep 303B notes on channel 3 and use a separate control-only MIDI Out on channel 6.     How do project Save, Save Default and Apply Default differ?

    How do I undo an edit?

    Hold HOME and press REC for Undo; hold HOME and press PLAY for Redo. A 303A high-pass edit was physically checked through edit, Undo, Redo and final Undo, with the other strip settings unchanged. This does not claim every action has identical Undo coverage. To clear one part's sequence, select that part first, then hold SAVE and press REC. Check that the confirmation names the intended part and current pattern; press SEL to confirm or HOME to cancel. This was exercised on 303A in P1: the device reported CLEARED 303A and the note display became empty. Do not use the global CLEAR PATTERN action when you only want to clear one part. Clearing notes does not reset the selected sound or diagnose a sustained/overload-like sound; those require separate checks.

    What does EXPAND mean, and why is returning to zero refused?

    EXPAND upgrades the project's timing storage to the expanded rate format. The device also initializes independent lanes if required. Existing notes and starting timing are retained; it does not generate or extend notes automatically. It is currently a one-way conversion: requesting zero is deliberately refused. The rotary 0/1 presentation and generic RATE CHANGE REFUSED message are misleading; clearer action/status wording is a recorded UI issue, not a fix already present. Preserve a private project backup before conversion. Older firmware cannot be assumed to load the expanded format.

    Why do all drum rows appear shortened when only the kick length changes?

    Known display issue: the grid uses the selected drum's length to shade all rows. During this device test, BD was set to 6 steps, SD to 8, and reselecting BD still showed 6. Thus the settings were independent even though the shading suggested otherwise. A per-row rendering correction is recorded for review. Subsequently, switching between BD and SD during internal playback showed their position indicators cycling independently over six and eight steps. This is physical visual timing evidence; a subsequent explicit Save and normal power cycle retained BD at six steps and SD at eight, both at 1/16. Measured audio/MIDI onset timing and all-lane persistence remain unverified.

    Why do all drum rows appear to share a playhead?

The requested correction is recorded for review; independent kick/snare timing settings, selected-lane cycling and persistence were checked separately. A shared display highlight is not a measurement of each audio clock.

    Does the delay already filter its repeats?

A separately adjustable feedback high-pass and explicit low-pass cutoff are suggestions for review; filtering alone is not a guarantee against excessive feedback.
